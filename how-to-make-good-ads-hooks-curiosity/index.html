How to make ads that convert with hooks and curiosity gap

A good hook makes the right person want the next sentence. A curiosity gap gives them a reason to stay.

A collection of tutorials, creative breakdowns, and brand examples. What to notice, why it holds attention, and how to use the idea in your own ads.

The short version: eight ideas to keep in mind
  1. Start with partial knowledge

    Establish something familiar, then introduce the missing piece.

  2. Align the first three seconds

    Make the picture, spoken line, and on-screen text tell the same opening idea.

  3. Make a promise

    Give the viewer a question or problem the rest of the ad will resolve.

  4. Keep the setup clear

    Curiosity works when the viewer understands why the answer matters.

  5. Build a testing system

    Vary hooks, explanations, and calls to action so you can learn what changes results.

  6. Show the outcome

    Make the desired change visible through a scene, a use case, or a demonstration.

  7. Recognize, then reveal

    Begin with a thought the buyer knows, challenge it, and earn the payoff.

  8. Keep a consistent point of view

    Use restraint and repetition to build recognition across different executions.
