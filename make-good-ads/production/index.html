02 / Production

From brief to finished ad

Research the buying situation. Choose a promise. Make the evidence visible. Deliver something we can learn from.

Research & briefConceptsFormat & lengthWorked assignmentDelivery

01 / Find the material before writing the line.

Read reviews, support conversations, interviews, search questions, and competitor offers. Look for the moment someone starts looking, what they tried before, and what makes them hesitate.

Build a small evidence sheet: customer observation → source → possible promise → evidence available → unanswered question. Mark assumptions so they can be challenged.

A useful observation versus a guess

Observation: several interviewees describe checking the lid before putting a bottle beside a laptop. Hypothesis: confidence in carrying it may be a stronger angle than hydration reminders. Missing evidence: whether the product performs under the conditions the proposed ad would show.

Fictional example. Do not present invented interviews as customer research.

02 / Agree on the assignment.

The creator owns the creative argument. The media buyer or business owner confirms the outcome, acquisition economics, measurement, and test question. Resolve missing proof and offer terms before filming.

03 / Separate the idea from its variations.

LevelMeaningFictional bottle assignment
AngleThe buying reason or perspective.Carry a bottle beside your work things with confidence.
ConceptThe central creative idea expressing the angle.Follow one bottle through an ordinary commute.
ExecutionThe specific visual and verbal treatment.A narrated bag-packing demonstration, filmed from above.
Hook variationA different entrance into substantially the same argument.A damp sleeve opening versus the bottle being packed beside the laptop.

Changing five opening lines is not the same as testing five concepts. Document what changes so the result can inform the next creative choice.

04 / Build the argument, then edit it.

Consider an opening, product relevance, demonstration or explanation, evidence, the main objection, the offer, and the action. Combine or reorder those jobs where useful. A static can do several in one frame; a video does not need a separate scene for each.

Read the script aloud. Review it as pictures without the voice-over. Ask what a viewer understands before they decide whether to continue. Remove every beat that neither clarifies the offer nor makes it more believable.

05 / Choose the format for the job.

There is no universal winning length or format in this guide. Choose a communication job, build a plausible execution, and compare the business outcome under a suitable test.

OptionUseful starting hypothesisWhat to check
StaticA proposition, offer, comparison, or proof point can be understood in one frame.Reading order, product recognition, mobile legibility, and destination match.
6–15 second videoOne compact action or demonstration can make the point.Does the opening establish context, and does the ad still deliver its promise?
15–30 second videoThe idea needs a short explanation, proof, or objection response.Does each additional beat contribute to the argument?
30–60+ second videoA considered offer needs a longer demonstration, narrative, or evaluation.Is the detail useful, or is the edit delaying a point that could be made sooner?

These are illustrative production bands to test. They are not performance benchmarks, placement limits, or claims that a particular length is best.

Short versus long? Static versus video?

A static can communicate a clear argument without waiting for a reveal. Video is useful when movement, time, a person, or a sequence adds evidence or meaning. Longer video is justified by useful information, not by the product’s price alone.

For a format comparison, hold the offer, promise, destination, and measurement as consistent as possible. Either compare purpose-built executions as complete packages or isolate a narrower change. State which question the test answers; different formats cannot be made identical in every respect.

A video completion rate is not comparable to a static’s engagement rate as evidence of sales effectiveness. Compare the shared business outcome, then use format-specific metrics to investigate why.

Fit the placement, not just the file.

Platform guidance

For Reels, Meta recommends vertical 9:16 video, intentional audio, and key information clear of interface overlays. Build captions and visual context so the proposition is still understandable without sound. Those execution recommendations do not establish that video beats static across every Meta placement.

Preview each intended placement on a phone. Check the opening frame, product, headline, captions, CTA, and disclosures against the interface. Reframe Feed and full-screen assets deliberately rather than assuming an automatic crop preserves the argument.

A worked assignment: one commute, one promise.

Fictional assignment · not a real product claim

Buyer situation: someone carries a bottle beside work equipment. Angle: confidence in an ordinary work bag. Promise: a bottle designed for that situation. Evidence required: a demonstration consistent with what the product can actually substantiate.

Concept C01: follow the bottle through a commute. Execution E01: overhead bag packing, the journey, and unpacking. Opening H01: introduce the laptop and bottle together so the concern is immediately clear.

BeatPicture / wordsWhy it is there
ContextA bottle is packed beside a laptop. “They share the same bag.”Establish the situation and product before asking for more attention.
DemonstrationShow how the lid closes and the conditions of the journey.Connect the product detail to the promise. Do not imply an edited scene proves every condition.
ResolutionUnpack the bag and show the result honestly, with the brand visible.Answer the question the opening introduced and connect it to the advertiser.
Action“See the lid, size, and care details.” Link to that product page.Offer the information needed for the next decision.

First production option: a 15–30 second demonstration. Static alternative: one clearly photographed bag-packing scene with a specific supported headline and product identifier. Test question: does the commute angle deserve more investment than the existing concept? A later format test can investigate the treatment.

How the same workflow changes for another category

Considered purchase: a mattress buyer worries about disturbing a partner. The brief needs relevant movement evidence, accurate trial terms, and the conditions of the demonstration.

Service: a cleaning customer wants to know what the quoted price includes. A checklist-led concept may answer the uncertainty better than a beautiful room reveal. Judge qualified bookings and subsequent cancellations, not just leads.

06 / Deliver a testable package.

Ship approved finals with editable files, captions, thumbnails, ad copy, destinations, and claim sources. The manifest should let the buyer identify the promise and changed element without guessing from the filename.
